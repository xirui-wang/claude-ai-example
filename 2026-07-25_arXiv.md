## Extended Summaries: 2D Materials & Microwave Engineering

### [2D] Huge hole injection in tungsten dichalcogenide heterostructures without electric gating: a DFT study — [2607.20032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20032)
This DFT study examines charge transfer at interfaces between different tungsten dichalcogenides (WS₂, WSe₂, WTe₂) in van der Waals heterostructures. The calculations reveal that massive spontaneous hole transfer occurs at the WX₂ interface without any externally applied gate voltage, driven purely by band alignment and interface dipole effects. This eliminates the need for electrostatic gating to achieve large carrier densities in 2D field-effect transistor architectures. The results provide a design principle for engineering 2D heterostructures with intrinsic, high-density hole populations relevant to p-type transistors and correlated-electron device applications.

### [2D] Valley polarization, Rashba interaction, and weak altermagnetism in inversion-asymmetric MnPS₃|WS₂ van der Waals heterostructures — [2607.16454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16454)
This first-principles study investigates a van der Waals heterostructure formed by stacking the magnetic insulator MnPS₃ on monolayer WS₂. Breaking inversion symmetry at the interface activates both valley polarization (from WS₂'s spin-valley locking) and Rashba spin-orbit coupling, while magnetic proximity from MnPS₃ induces a weak altermagnetic splitting in WS₂'s bands. The simultaneous presence of all three effects — valley polarization, Rashba, and altermagnetism — in a single 2D heterostructure is unprecedented and offers new degrees of freedom for valleytronics and spintronic device design. The authors identify specific stacking configurations that optimize each effect independently, providing experimental guidance.

### [2D] Two-Dimensional Hexagonal BX₃ (X=P, As) Monolayers as High-Capacity, Fast-Charging Anode Platforms for Lithium- and Sodium-Ion Batteries — [2607.15514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15514)
Using DFT, the authors evaluate hexagonal monolayers of BP₃ and BAs₃ as novel 2D anode materials for both Li- and Na-ion batteries. These materials exhibit theoretical specific capacities far exceeding graphite, combined with very low ion diffusion barriers that enable fast charging. Electronic structure calculations confirm metallic character upon lithiation/sodiation, ensuring good electronic conductivity throughout cycling. Mechanical stability analysis shows the monolayers remain dynamically stable under full ion intercalation, suggesting practical viability as next-generation battery electrodes.

### [2D] Research progress in high-pressure tuning of layered magnetic materials — [2607.13632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13632)
This review surveys how hydrostatic and uniaxial pressure modifies interlayer exchange coupling and magnetic ordering in van der Waals magnets such as CrI₃, CrBr₃, Fe₃GeTe₂, and related compounds. Pressure shortens interlayer distances and modifies the superexchange pathways, enabling continuous tuning of magnetic anisotropy and in some cases driving transitions between ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic interlayer coupling. The authors also discuss pressure-induced structural phase transitions that can fundamentally alter the electronic and magnetic ground state, and highlight how pressure complements electrostatic gating as a tuning knob unavailable through chemical substitution. Emerging directions include combining pressure with optical probes for in situ detection of magnetization switching in few-layer flakes.

### [2D] Chiral charge density waves in transition metal dichalcogenides — [2607.10182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10182)
This theoretical study by Ettore Carpene and colleagues predicts a new class of chirality-resolved CDW phases in layered transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs). When the CDW order breaks both translational and mirror symmetry simultaneously, it generates a chiral crystal structure that exhibits optical activity — circularly polarized light is absorbed differently depending on the CDW handedness. The work provides selection rules based on the TMD point group and identifies 1T-TaS₂ and NbSe₂ polytypes as primary candidates. These chiral CDW phases are significant because they would create a new, all-electronic route to chiroptical activity in inorganic 2D materials.

### [2D] Emergent ferromagnetism in the NiI₂–NbSe₂ van der Waals heterostructure — [2607.21218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21218)
This scanning tunnelling microscopy/spectroscopy (STM/STS) study at 5 K reveals that when the helimagnetic insulator NiI₂ is placed on the charge-density-wave superconductor NbSe₂, the RKKY-like coupling mediated by NbSe₂ quasiparticles suppresses the native helimagnetic ordering of NiI₂. Instead, a ferromagnetic state is stabilized at the interface, directly imprinting on the Yu-Shiba-Rusinov edge states observed by STS. This emergent interfacial ferromagnetism would not exist in either material alone and demonstrates that engineering magnetic order in 2D heterostructures through substrate-mediated coupling is a viable strategy. The hybridized YSR states further suggest the potential for topological superconductivity in this vdW platform.

### [2D] Contrasting Γ- and K-Valley Moiré Physics in Twisted Monolayer/Bilayer WSe₂ — [2607.18212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18212)
Using a combination of first-principles DFT and continuum moiré models, this work reveals that twisted monolayer/bilayer WSe₂ hosts two competing sets of flat bands at the Γ valley and K valley of the Brillouin zone, controlled by the twist angle and displacement field. The Γ-valley flat bands support Wigner crystal phases and charge-ordered Mott insulators due to their spatial localization at AA-stacked regions, while K-valley flat bands — accessed at larger angles — host fractional quantum anomalous Hall states with large Berry curvature. Identifying which valley governs the physics requires characterizing the displacement-field response of the moiré spectrum, providing a concrete experimental handle. This work unifies seemingly contradictory experimental reports of WSe₂ moiré physics by attributing them to different valley regimes.

### [2D] Raman spectroscopy of van der Waals topological magnet GdGaI — [2607.19663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19663)
This polarization-resolved Raman spectroscopy study characterizes the layered topological magnet GdGaI, a material predicted to host anomalous Hall conductivity and non-trivial band topology in addition to magnetic order. The measurements identify characteristic zone-center phonon modes and their temperature dependence across the magnetic ordering transition, revealing anomalous softening and linewidth broadening attributable to magnon-phonon coupling. Certain Raman-active modes exhibit strong polarization anisotropy consistent with the in-plane symmetry breaking at the magnetic transition, providing a contactless probe of the magnetic order parameter. The results establish Raman spectroscopy as a sensitive tool for characterizing the interplay between topology and magnetism in new vdW magnetic materials.

### [2D] Contrasting Γ- and K-Valley Moiré Physics: nonlinear Hall effect in Floquet-driven monolayer 1T'-MoS₂ — [2607.03717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03717)
This theoretical work studies how periodic laser driving (Floquet engineering) of monolayer 1T'-MoS₂ modifies its Berry curvature dipole and thereby the nonlinear Hall current. The Floquet sidebands hybridize the topologically non-trivial bands near the Weyl points, periodically enhancing and suppressing the Berry curvature dipole at the laser frequency. This enables a time-periodic, optically switchable nonlinear Hall current that can be toggled between large positive and negative values without any structural modification. The result identifies Floquet driving as a versatile, ultrafast handle for controlling non-linear transport in 2D topological materials.

### [2D] Terahertz field-driven nonlinear Hall effect and other second-order transport phenomena in two-dimensional tellurene — [2607.09256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09256)
Two-dimensional α-tellurium (tellurene) lacks inversion symmetry and possesses a large Berry curvature dipole, making it an ideal platform for Berry-phase-driven nonlinear transport. This work computes the THz-driven nonlinear Hall conductivity, photogalvanic, and gyrotropy responses, demonstrating that they are all related by a common Berry curvature dipole tensor despite arising from different perturbative channels. Strong THz fields generate measurable transverse voltage signals even at room temperature given tellurene's large intrinsic SOC. The study provides a quantitative transport map that guides the design of THz detectors and rectifiers based on 2D materials without magnetic order.

### [2D] Competing Chern states revealed by quasiparticle charging in moiré rhombohedral graphene — [2607.08710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08710)
Using single-electron addition spectroscopy in a scanning single-electron transistor geometry, this experiment maps the quantum capacitance spectrum of a moiré device formed from rhombohedral-stacked multilayer graphene. Incompressible states at fractional and integer moiré fillings are identified as Chern insulators with C=1 and C=2, and the measurements directly reveal how these phases compete with each other as a function of displacement field. The charging spectrum shows level crossings where the Chern number changes, providing a spectroscopic fingerprint of topological phase transitions inaccessible by bulk transport. These results demonstrate that rhombohedral graphene moiré systems host an unusually rich phase diagram of competing topological states.

### [2D] Twist-configured moiré-moiré reconstruction governs diverse commensurate double-moiré phases in twisted bilayer graphene on h-BN — [2607.02822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02822)
This computational work performs full atomistic relaxation of twisted bilayer graphene (tBG) placed on hexagonal boron nitride (hBN), generating a double-moiré superlattice. The simulations show that depending on the relative twist between the tBG and hBN moiré patterns, the system reconstructs into either a helical or alternating commensurate phase, with drastically different local stacking distributions. The reconstructed double-moiré fundamentally alters the flat-band spectrum compared to an unrelaxed calculation, shifting the magic-angle condition and generating additional satellite flat bands at otherwise unrecognized commensurate angles. These findings explain experimental variability in tBG/hBN devices and establish reconstruction as an essential ingredient in modeling double-moiré systems.

### [2D] Fourth-order Optoelectronic Response from Cascaded Circular Photogalvanic and Nonlinear Hall Effects — [2607.10012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10012)
This theoretical work derives a new class of fourth-order optical conductivity in 2D materials arising from a cascade of second-order processes: first a circular photogalvanic current is generated, and this photocurrent then drives a nonlinear Hall response to yield a fourth-order transverse signal. Both steps are mediated by Berry curvature and its dipole moment, so the combined response is non-perturbatively large in materials with strong SOC and broken inversion symmetry. The authors predict this effect is observable in monolayer TMDs and topological surface states under mid-infrared illumination with modest laser fluences. The work opens a route to high-order nonlinear optoelectronics in Berry-curvature-rich 2D platforms.

### [2D] Three-Dimensional Non-Foliated Fractional Quantum Hall Phases with Irrational Anyons in Twisted van der Waals Multilayers — [2607.13127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13127)
This theoretical study constructs a new family of 3D topological phases by coupling twisted van der Waals 2D layers into a bulk stack. Unlike conventional 3D fractional quantum Hall phases built from decoupled 2D layers (the "foliated" construction), these non-foliated phases exhibit irrational anyon braiding statistics that cannot be decomposed layer by layer. The interlayer coupling that generates the 3D phase arises from a carefully chosen set of electron tunnelling terms that simultaneously project onto the Landau level structure of each layer. The resulting anyons have irrational mutual statistics, making them candidates for fault-tolerant topological quantum computation beyond the standard abelian anyon paradigm.

### [2D] Stacking-tuned superconductivity and competing charge-density-wave states in NbSe₂ — [2607.20335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20335)
This work focuses on the 4Ha polytype of NbSe₂, in which H- and T-type NbSe₂ layers alternate, creating a distinct stacking environment compared to the well-studied 2H polytype. The authors find two competing CDW orders — a commensurate unidirectional CDW and an incommensurate triangular CDW — whose relative stability is tuned by the layer sequence, qualitatively reshaping the superconducting phase diagram. Stacking thus acts as a discrete structural knob that controls both CDW competition and the superconducting transition temperature, a mechanism unavailable in single-polytype crystals. These results highlight polytypism in layered TMDs as an underexplored dimension for engineering quantum phase competition.

### [2D] A unified tight-binding description of the electronic structure and Ising protection of superconductivity in misfit layered compounds — [2607.19308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19308)
Misfit layered compounds consist of alternating TMD layers and tetragonal (e.g., NbS, PbS) layers, forming a natural heterostructure without the need for external assembly. This study derives a DFT-parameterized tight-binding model for these compounds and shows that the intervening tetragonal layers mediate a previously neglected interlayer spin-orbit coupling that is absent in a naive rigid-band picture of the constituent TMD. This hidden SOC provides Ising protection to the superconducting order in the TMD layers, analogous to the protection seen in monolayer NbSe₂, but extending it to bulk 3D crystals. The finding explains the anomalously high in-plane upper critical fields observed in misfit compounds and provides a unified framework for engineering Ising superconductivity in 2D-like heterostructures.

### [2D] Ising superconductivity and anomalous metallic states in a bulk crystal with artificial unidirectional stacking layers — [2607.01302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01302)
This paper engineers a bulk crystal where all van der Waals layers are stacked in the same orientation (unidirectional stacking), breaking the inversion symmetry between layers that normally averages out in-plane spin-orbit fields. The resulting bulk system behaves electronically as a stack of 2D Ising superconductors, with in-plane upper critical fields far exceeding the Pauli limit. Simultaneously, anomalous metallic (failed-insulator) states are observed at low magnetic fields, coexisting with the superconducting phase and suggesting quantum fluctuation-driven vortex dynamics. The work establishes artificial unidirectional stacking as a scalable route to 3D Ising SC behaviour and anomalous metal physics outside the monolayer limit.

### [2D] Energetics of fractional anomalous Hall crystals in rhombohedral graphene — [2607.08822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08822)
This exact-diagonalization study examines the competition between fractional Chern insulator (FCI) states and anomalous Hall crystal (AHC) phases in rhombohedral multilayer graphene moiré devices. As the moiré wavelength increases, the Coulomb interaction energy scale relative to the bandwidth shifts the ground state from FCI — which has topological order and fractional statistics — to the AHC, which spontaneously breaks translation symmetry but retains integer Hall conductance. The study maps the phase boundary precisely and identifies specific fillings where the two phases are nearly degenerate, predicting a susceptibility to disorder-induced phase mixing. These results are directly relevant to recent experiments reporting anomalous Hall crystallization in pentalayer graphene moiré devices.

### [MW] On-chip quantum sensing of Kondo spins in a high-mobility quasi-one-dimensional nanoconstriction — [2607.13397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13397)
This experiment implements microwave impedance spectroscopy on a gate-defined GaAs quantum wire to detect the Kondo resonance of dilute magnetic impurities with single-spin sensitivity. The microwave probe couples to the wire's complex impedance, which carries a signature of the Kondo many-body resonance as a function of temperature and gate voltage. The technique achieves single-spin sensitivity by exploiting the high quality factor of on-chip superconducting microwave resonators coupled capacitively to the quantum wire. This on-chip microwave sensing approach is compatible with dilution refrigerator integration and demonstrates a scalable path toward real-time monitoring of local magnetic moments in mesoscopic devices.

### [MW] Pseudo-superconducting-diode effect in ferroelectric Josephson junctions — [2607.04398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04398)
This paper demonstrates a superconducting diode effect in a Josephson junction where the barrier is a ferroelectric thin film rather than a magnetic layer. Switching the ferroelectric polarization reverses the diode polarity by modifying the asymmetry of Andreev bound state energies without applying any magnetic field. Gate-voltage control of the ferroelectric state enables all-electrical, non-volatile toggling of the diode rectification direction. This field-free, gate-programmable Josephson diode is directly applicable to microwave superconducting circuits requiring non-reciprocal signal routing, such as quantum-limited amplifiers and single-photon detectors.

### [MW] Vortex-Number-Controlled Josephson Diode Polarity in Corbino Junctions — [2607.04104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04104)
In a Corbino (annular) Josephson junction geometry, the number of trapped Abrikosov vortices in the superconducting electrodes determines the current-phase relationship asymmetry. This work demonstrates that incrementing or decrementing the vortex count by one unit deterministically flips the superconducting diode polarity — the direction of preferred current flow — because each vortex introduces a 2π phase winding that shifts the $I_c^+/I_c^-$ balance. The effect is highly reproducible and can be programmed magnetically, making the vortex number a stable memory variable for diode polarity. This mechanism is distinct from spin-orbit-based diodes and offers a complementary route to building non-reciprocal elements in superconducting microwave circuits without requiring strong spin-orbit materials.
