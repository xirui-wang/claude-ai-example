## Extended Summaries: 2D Materials & Microwave Engineering

### [2607.21141] End-State-Controlled Quantum Transport in Armchair Graphene Nanoribbon Artificial Quantum Materials
*D. M. T. Kuo*

This work addresses a fundamental challenge in graphene nanoribbon (GNR) engineering: how the interplay between triangulene zero-energy modes and armchair GNR end states shapes transport. Using a real-space tight-binding framework, Kuo shows that at three-arm junctions these localized states hybridize into compact localized node orbitals (CLOs) — topologically robust building blocks for artificial GNR superlattices. The CLOs give rise to highly tunable flat subbands near the Fermi energy whose degeneracy can be controlled by junction design. The key result is that end-state engineering is sufficient to produce strongly anisotropic quantum transport coefficients, pointing toward atomically precise graphene devices with on-demand conductance channels.

### [2607.18212] Contrasting Γ- and K-Valley Moiré Physics in Twisted Monolayer/Bilayer WSe₂
*Submitted July 2026*

Twisted monolayer/bilayer WSe₂ (tML/BL-WSe₂) is identified as a rare moiré platform where a single displacement-field knob selects whether the dominant flat band derives from the K valley (spin-orbit-coupled, strongly spin-momentum locked) or the Γ valley (strongly coupled to phonons, with distinct orbital character). The paper maps out contrasting correlated insulator phases at moiré filling ν = 1: the K-valley regime shows a weak antiferromagnetic insulator near a van Hove singularity, reminiscent of twisted bilayer WSe₂, while the Γ-valley regime yields a qualitatively different ground state. At ν = 1/3 fractional filling, the two valleys again support distinct strongly correlated phases, offering a single material platform to benchmark competing theories of correlated electron physics in 2D moiré systems.

### [2607.13873] Superconducting Proximity Effect in a Strongly Correlated Charge-Transfer Insulator
*M. Ren, Y. Chen et al.*

Proximity-induced superconductivity at a lateral interface between two monolayer phases of NbSe₂ — superconducting H-NbSe₂ and charge-transfer insulating T-NbSe₂ — is studied with ultralow-temperature STM (< 100 mK). In the weak-coupling limit the T-NbSe₂ acquires a coherence gap smaller than that of H-NbSe₂, consistent with a proximity effect competing against Coulomb blockade from strong correlations. Strikingly, in the strong-coupling regime the gap in T-NbSe₂ approaches that of H-NbSe₂ while also developing pronounced particle-hole asymmetry, a fingerprint of the charge-transfer insulator background. This study establishes atomically sharp lateral junctions of 2D NbSe₂ phases as a model system for engineering and probing quantum states at the boundary between superconductivity and strong correlations, with potential relevance for topological quantum circuits.

### [2607.03908] Surface Functionalization Enables Two-Dimensional Altermagnetism and Giant Tunnel Magnetoresistance
*Z. Cui, Z. Zhu, B. Hao, X. Duan, X. Zhou, Y. Xie, H. Yang, B. Sa, R. Li, T. Zhou*

This study exploits a symmetry argument to propose a nonvolatile chemical route to 2D altermagnetism: surface functionalization of monolayer FeSe with H, O, or F atoms breaks inversion and out-of-plane mirror symmetries while preserving the rotation symmetry that enforces compensated magnetism, transforming a conventional antiferromagnet into a d-wave altermagnetic material. First-principles calculations confirm pronounced momentum-dependent spin splitting without net magnetization. Crucially, this symmetry-engineered altermagnetic state supports giant tunnel magnetoresistance ratios up to ~1870% in FeSe magnetic tunnel junctions — orders of magnitude larger than those in conventional antiferromagnetic barriers — making surface-functionalized 2D altermagnets promising candidates for non-volatile spintronic memory cells.

### [2607.08668] Microwave Studies of Single Crystal TeO₂ at Cryogenic Temperatures
*T. Holt, M. Goryachev, W. Campbell, M. E. Tobar (University of Western Australia)*

Single-crystal TeO₂ is characterized as a microwave dielectric from room temperature down to 20 mK using whispering-gallery-mode (WGM) resonators and finite-element modelling. The anisotropic permittivities are extracted as ε∥ = 25.75 ± 0.08 and ε⊥ = 20.90 ± 0.07, and cryogenic loss measurements reveal quality factors as high as Q ≈ 9 × 10⁶ with minimum loss tangent tan δ ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁸ at 20 mK — placing TeO₂ among the best-performing low-loss dielectrics at millikelvin temperatures. Electron spin resonance spectroscopy confirms a clean spin environment with only known background spin systems, relevant for dark-matter axion search cavities and quantum information applications. The combination of high Q, large permittivity, and optical activity makes TeO₂ an attractive single-crystal resonator material for cryogenic microwave quantum circuits.

### [2607.18916] Bridging Distributed Quantum Materials via Multi-Hotspot Vacuum: Remote Cooper Pairing and Andreev Teleportation
*Z. Lin, Z. Song, C. Xiao, W. Yao*

This theoretical work proposes a new class of non-local correlated quantum system: multiple mesoscopic conductors embedded in the individual split gaps of a multi-gap THz split-ring resonator (SRR), where each gap hosts a deep-subwavelength electromagnetic hotspot. The vacuum fluctuations of the cavity photon mode span all gaps simultaneously, mediating a retarded current-current interaction that bridges the spatially separated conductors (millimetres apart) into a single equilibrium many-body state. Applying this architecture to two metallic moiré superlattices — each proximitized to a THz SRR gap — the authors show that virtual photon exchange drives remote Cooper pair formation between carriers in the two distinct superlattices and supports Andreev teleportation across the macroscopic vacuum. This demonstrates that photonic vacuum fluctuations can replace wiring as a coupling medium in distributed quantum material devices, opening a route to cavity-mediated entanglement of widely separated correlated electron systems.
