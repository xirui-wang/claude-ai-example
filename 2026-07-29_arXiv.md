## Extended Summaries: 2D Materials & Microwave Engineering

### [Observation of correlation-driven topological transport and robust ferromagnetism in 2D CrS₂](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23625)
*2607.23625 — Sk Md Obaidulla, M. Nur Hasan, Dayal Das, Rafiqul Alam, Antonio Supina, Muhammad Awais Aslam, Sherif Kamal, Iva Šarić Jankovic, Aleksandar Matkovic, Christian Teichert, Atindra Nath Pal, Heike C. Herper, Marko Kralj*

This work reports the first catalyst-free CVD synthesis of the layered ferromagnet 1T-CrS₂ — a highly stable van der Waals material with out-of-plane magnetic anisotropy and a Curie temperature exceeding room temperature. Transport measurements reveal a semimetal–insulator crossover near 80 K alongside pronounced negative magnetoresistance up to 350 K, signaling strong spin-disorder scattering. Most strikingly, a topological Hall effect emerges below 30 K, indicating noncoplanar spin textures at low temperature that generate a real-space Berry phase. The work is significant because 2D ferromagnets with room-temperature ordering and emergent topological transport are rare, making 1T-CrS₂ an attractive platform for van der Waals spintronics and topological device architectures.

### [Large-area CVD Graphene for Photogating-Based Photodetection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24344)
*2607.24344 — D. A. Matienko, D. P. Borisenko, A. Yu. Kuntsevich*

This study demonstrates photogating-based photodetection in mono-, bi-, and trilayer CVD graphene grown on standard SiO₂/Si wafers, a fully scalable and CMOS-compatible platform. The photoconductivity signal arises from substrate-induced charge trapping in the SiO₂ layer under super-bandgap illumination of the Si substrate, which shifts the graphene's Fermi level and modulates its conductance. Monolayer graphene achieves the highest responsivity of 995 A/W owing to minimal screening, while increasing layer count reduces sensitivity but lowers noise, improving detectivity. The approach requires no complex heterostructure engineering and naturally scales with wafer-level CVD graphene, making it immediately relevant for on-chip photo-sensing integrated circuits.

### [Stacking-tuned superconductivity and competing charge-density-wave states in NbSe₂](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20335)
*2607.20335 — Sandra Sajan, Xinze Yang, Haojie Guo, Tarushi Agarwal, Samuel Mañas-Valero, Carla Boix-Constant, Eugenio Coronado, Fernando de Juan, Eduardo H. da Silva Neto, Ravi P. Singh, Maria N. Gastiasoro, Miguel M. Ugeda*

Using scanning tunneling microscopy and spectroscopy, this work establishes that the stacking sequence in van der Waals NbSe₂ is a previously underappreciated control knob for collective order. The 4Ha polytype — differing from the well-studied 2H phase solely in layer registry — hosts two competing CDW ground states: a unidirectional commensurate 1Q^C phase and the triangular incommensurate 3Q^I phase known in 2H. This stacking-driven CDW reconstruction is accompanied by modifications to superconductivity, highlighting that competing quantum orders in 2D materials can be tuned without chemical doping or external strain. The findings position polytype engineering as a new route to control quantum phase competition in van der Waals superconductors.

### [Frequency-Division Multiplexing in Magnonic Waveguides](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20102)
*2607.20102 — Alina-Cristina Bunea, Laurentiu Stoleriu, Giacomo Talmelli, Dan Neculoiu, Christoph Adelmann, Florin Ciubotaru*

Magnonic logic promises wave-based computing at microwave frequencies with ultralow power consumption, but practical implementations require simultaneous multi-channel signal routing through shared media. This work provides the first direct experimental proof of frequency-division multiplexing in a CoFeB spin-wave waveguide: two independently generated microwave signals are coupled via inductive antennas, and vector network analyzer measurements confirm that spin waves of different frequencies and wavelengths co-propagate without measurable cross-channel interaction in the linear regime. The spectral independence of each channel means that physical channel capacity scales with bandwidth rather than with device count, laying critical groundwork for magnonic data buses. The results validate theoretical predictions for linear spin-wave multiplexing and mark a concrete step toward integrated magnonic transceiver circuits operating in the GHz range.

### [Magnetically memorable inductance in superconducting multilayer resonators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21147)
*2607.21147 — R. Tyumenev, D. S. Kalashnikov, B. V. Fradkin, A. A. Neilo, A. G. Shishkin, N. V. Klenov, I. I. Soloviev, A. A. Golubov, M. Yu. Kupriyanov, I. A. Golovchanskiy, V. S. Stolyarov, A. S. Sidorenko, S. V. Bakurskiy*

Superconductor-ferromagnet hybrids are appealing for non-volatile, field-free tuning of microwave circuit elements through their kinetic inductance, which reflects the Cooper-pair density modified by the exchange interaction. This work fabricates split-ring resonators from Nb/Co/Nb/Co/Nb/Al spin-valve multilayers and demonstrates that reversing the relative magnetization of the cobalt layers — switching between parallel and antiparallel alignment — produces a reproducible resonant frequency shift of up to 4 MHz at zero applied field. The shift corresponds to a measurable change in kinetic inductance without any external field, realizing a truly non-volatile microwave memory element. This approach is compatible with existing superconducting qubit and readout architectures and offers a pathway to magnetically programmable microwave filters and resonators for quantum computing and sensing applications.
