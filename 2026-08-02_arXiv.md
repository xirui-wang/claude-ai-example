## Extended Summaries: 2D Materials & Microwave Engineering

### Probing the Potential Profile of Twisted Bilayer Graphene via Fabry-Pérot Interference — [2607.27948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27948)
Curtis McDowell, Alina Mreńca-Kolasińska, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Ming-Hao Liu, Thiti Taychatanapat report anomalous resistance oscillations in large-angle twisted bilayer graphene (tBLG) in the nominally unipolar transport regime, tracing them to a hidden Fabry-Pérot cavity formed by local unintentional doping rather than the global graphene cavity. By analyzing the zero-field interference fringe spacing, the authors determine the location and spatial extent of this doping inhomogeneity with nanometer precision. Magnetotransport measurements distinguish local cavity modes from global graphene resonances through their distinct magnetic-field dependence. Numerical simulations using the extracted potential profile quantitatively reproduce the experimental oscillation features. This work highlights how local electrostatic disorder, invisible to bulk probes, controls mesoscale quantum-coherent transport in moiré 2D materials and has direct implications for the design of tBLG-based quantum devices.

### Effects of gold cluster intercalation in graphene: stationary waves and modified QPI features — [2607.28297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28297)
Cristina Bena, Marion Cranney, Poonam Kumari, Alberto Zobelli, and Laurent Simon study the electronic consequences of intercalating Au clusters beneath epitaxial graphene on SiC using Fourier-transform scanning tunneling spectroscopy (FT-STS). Each Au cluster sitting at a graphene hollow site creates a ring-like scattering potential coupling to the six surrounding carbon atoms; within a T-matrix framework, this geometry generates elliptical QPI features concentrated near the graphene M points rather than the K points typical of point scatterers. The same real-space T-matrix calculation naturally explains the nearly-stationary standing-wave patterns observed experimentally across multiple sample regions. These results demonstrate that the geometry and site symmetry of intercalated species are decisive in shaping quasiparticle interference in graphene, with implications for interpreting FT-STS in other intercalated 2D materials.

### Third-order nonlinear transport in a percolative two-dimensional superconductor — [2607.27641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27641)
Wenjun Liu, Chenghe Wang, Xiubin Li, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Tao Zhang, Xiao-Xiao Zhang, and Jing Li probe the nonlinear response of a trilayer 1T'-MoTe₂ device within its percolative superconducting transition regime, observing a prominent third-harmonic longitudinal voltage with a characteristic cubic dependence on excitation current below a threshold current. The magnitude and nonlinear coefficient are strongly correlated with the superconducting transition temperature, ruling out normal-state nonlinear effects as the origin. A time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau model of fluctuating Cooper pairs in a percolative medium semi-quantitatively captures both the magnitude and temperature dependence of the nonlinear coefficient. Published in Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 056201 (2026), this work opens a new window into the fluctuation physics of 2D superconductors and could be used to quantify the vortex-dominated transport in the crossover regime.

### Interaction-driven electronic ferroelectricity in van der Waals heterostructures — [2607.24087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24087)
Ziying Wang, Ana Vera Montoto, Mohammad Amini, Yuxiao Ding, Jose L. Lado, Robert Drost, Adolfo O. Fumega, and Peter Liljeroth realize a new class of ferroelectric order in bilayer 1T-TaSe₂, where two stacking geometries (A-C and A-C') formed by coupled Star-of-David charge density wave (CDW) phases break inversion symmetry through electronic interactions alone, without any ionic distortion or interlayer sliding. Using MBE-grown samples with atomic-scale STM characterization and ab initio calculations, the authors map the energetics and polarization of the two stacking orders. This demonstrates that strong electronic correlations in narrow-band CDW systems can generate emergent ferroelectricity, providing a fundamentally new mechanism beyond lattice distortions, interlayer sliding, or moiré reconstruction commonly invoked for van der Waals ferroelectrics.

### High-Q superconducting microwave resonators using MBE titanium nitride — [2607.15230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15230)
Anand Ithepalli, Haoran Lu, Eegene Clara Chung, Xiangqin Wang, Amit Rohan Rajapurohita, Keun-Yeol Park, Celesta S. Chang, Peter McMahon, Huili Grace Xing, David Muller, Valla Fatemi, and Debdeep Jena demonstrate molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) of TiN on c-plane sapphire achieving an X-ray rocking curve width of 18 arcsec — the lowest reported for TiN — yielding quarter-wavelength coplanar waveguide (CPW) resonators with internal quality factors Q_int > 10⁶ at single-photon levels (5.8 GHz, 10 mK) and exceeding 20 × 10⁶ at high photon occupation. The authors identify, for the first time, sub-surface defects in the sapphire substrate that nucleate structural defects in the epitaxial TiN, providing a clear pathway for further improvement. These record Q values, enabled by the epitaxial quality of the TiN film, are directly relevant to quantum computing architectures that require low-loss microwave elements and demonstrate MBE-TiN as a competitive platform for superconducting qubit integration.
