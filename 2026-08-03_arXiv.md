## Extended Summaries: 2D Materials & Microwave Engineering

### Microwave Engineering

### [High-Q Superconducting Microwave Resonators Using MBE Titanium Nitride](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15230)
**arXiv:2607.15230 | Anand Ithepalli, Haoran Lu, Eegene Clara Chung, Xiangqin Wang, Amit Rohan Rajapurohita, Keun-Yeol Park, Celesta S. Chang, Peter McMahon, Huili Grace Xing, David Muller, Valla Fatemi, Debdeep Jena**

This work reports the highest-quality epitaxial titanium nitride (TiN) thin films achieved to date via molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) on c-plane sapphire, as evidenced by an X-ray rocking curve FWHM of only 18 arcsec — a new record for this material system. A key discovery is the identification, for the first time, of sub-surface crystallographic defects in the sapphire substrate that nucleate structural faults in the overlying TiN layer, pointing toward a new pathway for reducing microwave loss: improving substrate preparation rather than the deposition process alone. The TiN films are integrated into quarter-wavelength coplanar waveguide (CPW) resonators and tested at millikelvin temperatures, yielding internal quality factors exceeding 10⁶ in the single-photon regime at 5.8 GHz and surpassing 2×10⁷ at elevated photon numbers — results that place these resonators among the best reported for any superconducting material. These high-Q resonators are directly relevant to superconducting qubit applications, where minimizing two-level system losses at the material interface is essential for achieving long coherence times. The work also opens a path toward fully epitaxial Josephson junctions with crystalline tunnel barriers, a route toward more reproducible and scalable qubit fabrication.

### 2D Materials

### [Dual Enhancement of Superconductivity in FeSe/SrTiO₃ via Orbital and Correlation Synergy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27104)
**arXiv:2607.27104 | Guihao Jia, Jingming Yan, Yucong Peng, Shendong Su, Pei Ouyang, Xiaopeng Hu, Qi-Kun Xue, Wei Li | PNAS 123, e2602209123 (2026)**

Monolayer FeSe on SrTiO₃ is a canonical 2D high-temperature superconductor whose enhanced Tc relative to bulk FeSe remains a subject of active investigation. This study uses a scanning tunneling microscope tip to apply controlled tensile strain to the monolayer, revealing a previously unseen two-stage enhancement mechanism. In the first stage, in-plane lattice expansion amplifies electronic correlations, increasing the pairing gap from 17.8 meV progressively. As strain increases further, the normally inert dz² orbital band — located far below the Fermi level in unstrained FeSe — is pushed upward and begins to hybridize with the pairing-active dxy band, triggering a second, stronger enhancement that drives the gap to 23.6 meV. Throughout both stages, invariant Fermi wave vectors confirm that carrier density is unchanged, establishing that the effect is entirely attributable to band renormalization and orbital reconstruction rather than doping. This finding demonstrates that orbital degrees of freedom in FeSe/SrTiO₃ can be controllably activated by strain, offering a new design principle for engineering pairing strength in 2D van der Waals and interface superconductors.

### [Large-area CVD Graphene for Photogating-Based Photodetection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24344)
**arXiv:2607.24344 | (see arXiv page for full author list)**

This study demonstrates a scalable photodetection platform based on large-area chemical vapor deposition (CVD) graphene placed on a SiO₂/Si substrate, where the photogating mechanism — rather than intrinsic graphene photoconductivity — is shown to be the dominant photoresponse pathway. By systematically studying mono-, bi-, and trilayer CVD graphene devices, the authors confirm through gate-voltage-dependent resistivity measurements and direct comparison of capacitive recharging current with the photoresponse that silicon substrate photogeneration drives a charge redistribution in the SiO₂ layer, which then capacitively gates the graphene channel. The responsivity reaches up to 995 A/W for monolayer graphene, and the devices respond to photon energies above the silicon bandgap but not to telecom-frequency light (below Si gap), consistent with the photogating mechanism. The large sample area enabled precise statistical comparison of device-to-device uniformity, demonstrating that CVD graphene maintains this mechanism across macroscopic scales. This work establishes a clear design framework for high-responsivity graphene-on-Si photodetectors and confirms that photogating via the Si substrate is the key figure-of-merit to optimize, rather than the intrinsic photoconductivity of graphene itself.

### [Drumhead Surface States of Rhombohedral Graphite with Near Ideal Quantum Geometry Condition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28491)
**arXiv:2607.28491 | Lin-Lin Wang**

Rhombohedral graphite (RG), the multilayer stacking of graphene in the ABC arrangement, hosts flat drumhead surface states that are of intense interest as a platform for correlation-driven phases including surface superconductivity. This work applies density functional theory combined with Wannier tight-binding models to systematically characterize the topology of bulk RG and the quantum geometry of its drumhead surface states (DSS). The analysis shows that bulk RG is a weak topological insulator when spin-orbit coupling is included, and that thick RG slabs approach the ideal quantum geometry (IQG) condition — wherein the quantum metric and Berry curvature are equal in magnitude — a condition associated with maximally efficient flat-band superconductivity and fractional Chern insulator states. These findings provide a topological interpretation for the observed surface superconductivity in RG and suggest that the IQG condition in drumhead states could be a fundamental reason for the unusually high pairing strength seen experimentally, connecting the geometric properties of these 2D-like surface bands to the emergence of correlated phases.
