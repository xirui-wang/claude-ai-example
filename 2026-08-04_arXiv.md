## Extended Summaries: 2D Materials & Microwave Engineering

### Third-order nonlinear transport in a percolative two-dimensional superconductor — [2607.27641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27641)

This study explores nonlinear transport in the van der Waals superconductor 1T'-MoTe₂ in its percolative transition regime, where superconducting islands coexist with normal-state electrons. Using a trilayer device geometry with hBN encapsulation, the authors measure a third-harmonic longitudinal voltage that scales cubically with excitation current and is strongly correlated with the superconducting state. The nonlinear response is quantitatively captured by the time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory, with nonlinearity originating from fluctuating Cooper pairs in the percolative network. Importantly, the nonlinear coefficient tracks the evolution of superconducting correlations through the percolative transition, making third-harmonic transport a powerful and non-invasive probe of 2D superconducting fluctuations. The work establishes 1T'-MoTe₂ as an ideal 2D platform for studying fluctuation physics in atomically thin superconductors and suggests broader applicability to other 2D vdW superconductors with intrinsic inhomogeneity.

### Anomalous Localization in Magnetically Doped Two-Dimensional Topological Insulators — [2607.24373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24373)

Two-dimensional topological insulators (2DTIs) host spin-momentum-locked edge states protected by time-reversal symmetry, but magnetic impurities break this protection and open a route to backscattering. This work develops an analytical framework, validated by extensive numerical simulations, to predict the edge transport behavior in 2DTIs with finite concentrations of randomly distributed magnetic impurities. The key finding is an anomalous localization regime intermediate between the ballistic and Anderson-localized limits, characterized by a sub-exponential conductance decay ln G ∝ −√L with system length L. This behavior — distinct from both conventional metallic diffusion and exponential Anderson localization — arises from the interplay between the topological suppression of backscattering and disorder-induced magnetic dephasing. The results have direct implications for the design of magnetically doped quantum anomalous Hall systems and the onset of localization in 2D topological devices.

### Unconventional and Fragile Magnetic Exciton in a van der Waals Quantum Magnet — [2607.27695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27695)

The van der Waals antiferromagnet NiPS₃ hosts a sharp photoluminescence peak attributed to an entangled magnetic exciton whose optical brightness and narrow linewidth have attracted significant attention. This work uses hydrostatic pressure as a symmetry-preserving external tuning knob to probe the microscopic origin of this exciton. The remarkable finding is that the sharp PL peak is drastically suppressed by only 0.4 GPa and completely quenched by 1.5 GPa — far below the scale of structural phase transitions — revealing extreme fragility to perturbations of the exchange environment. The pressure dependence is explained within a framework of high-order perturbation theory rather than a first-order transition, suggesting the exciton's coherent brightness arises from either exciton pairing or spin-orbit coupling in a delicate quantum superposition. These results highlight NiPS₃ as a unique 2D quantum magnet where excitonic coherence is extremely sensitive to structural perturbations, making it a promising testbed for magneto-optical coupling in vdW heterostructures.

### Drumhead Surface States of Rhombohedral Graphite with Near Ideal Quantum Geometry Condition — [2607.28491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28491)

Rhombohedral graphite (ABC-stacked multilayer graphene) supports drumhead surface states — nearly flat bands localized at the surface that span the bulk nodal lines in the Brillouin zone. This study uses density functional theory and Wannier function analysis to characterize the bulk topology and surface states of semi-infinite rhombohedral graphite systems. A central finding is that the drumhead states closely satisfy the ideal quantum geometry (IQG) condition, where quantum metric and Berry curvature are of equal magnitude, a property theoretically favorable for realizing fractional Chern insulator states in flat bands. The work establishes that rhombohedral graphite is a weak topological insulator under spin-orbit coupling and an effective chiral semimetal with a chiral nodal line without SOC. These results suggest that rhombohedral graphite surfaces may provide a route to robust topological flat-band physics without the need for external twist engineering.
